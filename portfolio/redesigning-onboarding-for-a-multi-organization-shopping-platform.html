Redesigning Onboarding for a Multi-Organization Shopping Platform

Reframing account creation and pantry enrollment to make the path into an online food pantry shopping platform more flexible and understandable.

Role
Product Designer & UX Engineer
Focus
User flows, information architecture, UX writing, interaction design
Tools
Figma, application analytics, stakeholder interviews, support feedback

Overview

I redesigned the account onboarding experience for an online shopping platform used by food pantry clients and their shoppers.

The original flow treated account creation and pantry enrollment as a two-part process. After beginning registration, shoppers had to wait for an email and complete a second step before they could continue. Many users missed that step and became unable to access the shopping experience.

I helped replace this rigid process with a more flexible account model in which shoppers could maintain their own account and connect it to one or more participating pantries.

The challenge

The original onboarding flow assumed that a shopper’s identity was tied directly to a single pantry.

That assumption created both usability and product-model problems. Shoppers first entered their information, then waited for an email containing the next step. If they did not see or understand the message, onboarding stopped with little indication of how to recover.

The process created several points of friction:

  • Shoppers did not always recognize that registration was incomplete.
  • Email delays or missed messages interrupted the experience.
  • Users could not easily recover without assistance.
  • The account model did not accommodate shoppers who interacted with multiple pantries.
  • The product team had limited control over communication between individual pantries and their end users.

The visible usability problem was a failed registration flow. The deeper design problem was the relationship among shoppers, accounts, and pantry organizations.

Research and discovery

I used several sources to understand the problem:

  • Stakeholder and client interviews
  • Support requests
  • Observation of user behavior
  • Product analytics
  • Usability feedback
  • Review of the existing workflow and technical constraints

These inputs showed that missed emails were not simply a content problem. Improving the message alone would not address the underlying dependency on a delayed, two-stage process.

The redesign needed to reconsider the platform’s account structure.

My role

I contributed across the design and implementation process, including:

  • User and stakeholder research
  • Requirements gathering
  • Journey and workflow mapping
  • Information architecture
  • User-flow design
  • Wireframing and interface design
  • UX writing
  • Technical implementation

I did not own formal usability testing after the redesign, so I have kept the conclusions focused on the design process and resulting workflow rather than claiming unverified performance improvements.

The key design decision

The central challenge was defining the relationship between a shopper and a pantry.

Instead of treating the pantry as the owner of the shopper’s identity, I helped design a model in which:

  1. A shopper could create and maintain an individual account.
  2. The account could be connected to a pantry.
  3. The same shopper could later join additional participating pantries.
  4. Pantry-specific eligibility and operational rules could remain distinct from the shopper’s core identity.

This separation created a more flexible foundation while more accurately reflecting how shoppers interacted with the broader service ecosystem.

The redesigned experience

The revised workflow reduced reliance on a single, fragile handoff.

I reorganized onboarding so that account creation and pantry participation were clearer, related concepts rather than an opaque two-part registration process. The interface and UX copy needed to help shoppers understand:

  • What account they were creating
  • What joining a pantry meant
  • Whether their account or pantry connection was complete
  • What action they needed to take next
  • How they could connect with another pantry later

The design also needed to accommodate variation among pantries without making shoppers understand those organizational differences.

Constraints and tradeoffs

A major constraint was that the product team did not fully control communication between participating pantries and their end users.

Different organizations had their own processes, terminology, and expectations. The redesigned workflow therefore needed to be flexible enough for multiple operational models while still presenting shoppers with a coherent experience.

The new model also introduced complexity: supporting multiple pantry relationships required clearer distinctions among account-level information, pantry-specific information, and enrollment status.

My goal was not merely to reduce the number of steps. It was to make the underlying relationships understandable without exposing unnecessary system complexity.

Outcome

The redesigned onboarding workflow was eventually implemented.

Although I do not have formal test results or performance metrics, the new design addressed the central structural limitation of the original experience: shoppers were no longer defined solely through a one-to-one relationship with a pantry.

The project demonstrates my ability to:

  • Use qualitative and behavioral evidence to identify the cause of a UX problem
  • Separate a visible interface issue from the underlying product-model issue
  • Design for users who interact with multiple organizations
  • Translate complex account relationships into understandable user flows
  • Balance user needs, client operations, and technical constraints
  • Contribute from initial research through interface implementation

Reflection

The most important lesson from this project was that onboarding problems are often data-model and service-design problems in disguise.

The missed email was the clearest symptom, but it was not the root cause. By examining the relationship among shoppers, accounts, and pantries, I was able to help design a more flexible workflow rather than simply optimizing an unreliable handoff.
